Customer support SLA template

A ready-to-edit service level agreement for small support teams. It covers support hours, priority levels, response and resolution targets, when the clock pauses, and escalation. Replace everything in [brackets].

Customer Support Service Level Agreement (SLA)

Company: [Your company]
Effective date: [Date]
Applies to: support requests sent through [email address / help center / chat]

1. Support hours
Our support team works [Monday to Friday], [9:00 AM to 5:00 PM] [time zone], excluding [list holidays or link to holiday calendar]. All response times below are counted in these business hours.

2. Priority levels and response targets
Urgent: the service is down or unusable for most users.
  First response: within [1] business hour. Resolution target: within [1] business day.
High: a major feature is broken and there is no workaround.
  First response: within [4] business hours. Resolution target: within [2] business days.
Normal: a problem with a workaround, or a how-to question.
  First response: within [1] business day. Resolution target: within [5] business days.
Low: feature requests, cosmetic issues and general feedback.
  First response: within [2] business days. Resolution target: reviewed in our regular planning.

3. How priority is set
The customer can suggest a priority when they contact us. Our team confirms or adjusts it within the first response and explains any change.

4. What counts as a response
A first response is a reply from a person that confirms the issue and gives a next step. Automatic acknowledgements do not count.

5. When the clock pauses
The resolution clock pauses while we are waiting on information or access from the customer, and restarts when they reply.

6. Escalation
If a ticket misses its target, or the customer is unhappy with progress, it is escalated to [name / role] at [email address].

7. Reporting
We review SLA performance every [month] and share a summary with [customers on request / account managers].

8. Changes
We may update this SLA with [30] days' written notice.

How to use this template

  1. Start with targets you already meet. Pull last month's response times first. If you hit a target about 90% of the time, it's a good starting promise. Our SLA calculator works out your hit rate.
  2. Count in business hours. Unless you staff evenings and weekends, say so clearly. The calculator also shows exactly when a ticket is due.
  3. Define a "response". An automatic acknowledgement shouldn't stop the clock. Customers notice when it does.
  4. Pause while waiting on the customer. Otherwise slow customer replies count against you.
  5. Set it up in your help desk. If you use HubSpot, Service Hub can track first-response and time-to-close SLAs on tickets.
