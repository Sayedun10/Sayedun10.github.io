How to choose SLA targets

An SLA (service level agreement) is a promise about how fast you'll respond to or resolve a ticket. Most support teams set two:

  • First response time: how long until a person replies. An automatic "we got your email" doesn't count.
  • Time to close: how long until the issue is resolved.

Set targets by priority, and count them in business hours unless you staff nights and weekends. A starting point many small teams use:

PriorityFirst responseTime to close
Urgent (service down)1 business hour1 business day
High4 business hours2 business days
Normal1 business day5 business days
Low2 business days10 business days

These are example targets, not an industry standard. Start with targets you already meet about 90% of the time, then tighten them.

Setting SLAs in HubSpot

HubSpot Service Hub can set first-response and time-to-close SLAs on tickets, show due times in the help desk, and report on SLA status. Which plans include SLAs, and where the settings live, changes over time, so check HubSpot's knowledge base for "SLA" before you set them up. If you're also choosing a plan, try the Service Hub cost calculator.

Measure real handle time in HubSpot

Tally Time tracks how long your team actually works on each HubSpot ticket, automatically, and writes it to a ticket property you can report on. That's the handle time this calculator needs. It's free to install during beta.
