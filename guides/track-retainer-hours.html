How to track retainer hours (and stop giving away free work)

A retainer only works if you know how many hours you've used. Without tracking, work keeps going past the limit and the extra hours never get billed. This guide covers what to log, when to warn the client, how to handle rollover and overage, and how to set it up in HubSpot.

Want to start right now? Use our free retainer hours tracker. Add a client, log hours, and print a monthly report. It needs no sign-up.

1. Agree on what counts as retainer time

Most disputes about hours are really about scope. Write these down in the agreement before the first month starts:

  • What's included: for example support tickets, monthly reporting, small site updates.
  • What isn't: new projects, rush work and third-party costs are usually quoted separately.
  • The smallest unit you log: 15 minutes is common. It keeps quick emails from adding up to hours nobody tracked.
  • Whether meetings count. Most agencies count client calls. Internal meetings usually don't count.

2. Log time as you go, not at month end

Hours you reconstruct from memory at month end are usually lower than the real number. Log each piece of work the same day, with three things:

  • the date,
  • a short description the client would recognize ("Updated pricing page", not "misc"),
  • the hours.

3. Warn the client at 80%

An 80% warning gives both of you time to decide, before the hours run out, whether to pause, prioritize or approve extra time. A message at 100% feels like a bill nobody saw coming.

A simple message works: "Quick heads-up: we've used 16 of your 20 hours this month. The remaining 4 will cover the report and two support requests. Anything beyond that is billed at $150 an hour unless you'd like to hold it for next month."

4. Decide how rollover and overage work

PolicyHow it worksGood for
Use it or lose itUnused hours expire at month end.Steady monthly work. It's the simplest to run.
Capped rolloverUnused hours carry over, up to a limit (for example 25% of the retainer), for one month.Clients with uneven months. It's the most common compromise.
Overage billingHours past the limit are billed at an agreed hourly rate.Any retainer. Agree the rate in writing first.
Hours bankThe client prepays a block of hours, which you draw down until it's used.Irregular or project-like support.

5. Send a monthly hours report

A short report each month builds trust and makes renewals easier. Include hours used against the retainer, a list of the work done, any carried-over hours, and any overage. Our tracker prints this for you.

Tracking retainer hours in HubSpot

HubSpot has no built-in time tracking for tickets or retainers. The "Time tracking of tickets" request on the HubSpot Ideas forum has been open for years (see the thread). Teams usually choose one of these setups:

Option A: a manual property on tickets

  1. In HubSpot, click the settings icon, then go to Data Management > Properties.
  2. Choose Ticket properties and create a number property called "Time spent (hours)".
  3. Ask the team to fill it in before closing each ticket.
  4. Each month, export tickets and drop the file into our free ticket time report to get hours by company and month.

The catch: people forget, or guess at the end of the day. The numbers are only as good as everyone's memory.

Option B: a separate time tracker

Tools like Harvest, Clockify or Toggl track time well, but they live outside HubSpot. Someone has to match hours to HubSpot companies by hand each month.

Option C: automatic tracking inside HubSpot

Tally Time, built by us, tracks time automatically while your team works on HubSpot tickets. Each person confirms their time in one click. It shows retainer hours used on each company record, warns at 80%, and creates the monthly client report. It's free to install during the beta.

Not on HubSpot, or just want something simple? The free retainer hours tracker works in any browser and exports to Google Sheets or Excel.
